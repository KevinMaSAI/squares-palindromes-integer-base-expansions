\begin{abstract}
For a real number $x$ and an integer base $b\ge2$, let $H_b(x)$ be the normalized factor entropy of the canonical base-$b$ expansion of $x$. If $H_b(x)<1/2$, then for any finite list of nonzero rational slopes and any finite list of grouping widths, there is a common rational translation $\tau$ such that every image $\lambda x+\tau$ has bounded palindrome length in each corresponding grouped base. If $x$ is algebraic irrational, these images remain algebraic irrationals in the same number field. Thus the existence of an algebraic irrational with factor entropy strictly less than $1/2$ would yield a counterexample to the palindrome assertion in Bugeaud's Problem~10.42. No such algebraic example is available, and the original unbounded-length questions remain open. We also show that the threshold is sharp among real inputs: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a set of transcendental numbers of Hausdorff dimension at least $1/2$, with entropy in the interval $[1/2,1/2+\varepsilon)$, on which every rational affine image has arbitrarily long squares and even palindromes, while early disjoint repeats remain of logarithmic length.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

For an integer $b\ge2$ and a real number $x$, write $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$ and $\dist{x}=\min_{n\in\Z}|x-n|$. Let $\mathsf S_b(x)$ mean that the canonical base-$b$ expansion of the fractional part of $x$ contains factors $VV$ with $|V|$ unbounded. Let $\mathsf P_b(x)$ mean that it contains palindromes of unbounded length. Bugeaud's Problem~10.42 asks whether each property holds for every real algebraic irrational $x$ and every integer $b\ge2$ \cite[p.~220]{Bugeaud}. Adamczewski and Rampersad posed the corresponding questions in base two \cite{AR}.

The digit block starting after $i$ places and of length $d$ has integer value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:digitblock}
 D_{i,d}(x)=\lfloor b^{i+d}x\rfloor-b^d\lfloor b^i x\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Leading zeros are included. For a word $W$ let $[W]_b$ denote its integer value. Deleting any fixed prefix preserves both $\mathsf S_b$ and $\mathsf P_b$. A long palindrome can be trimmed symmetrically. If all unbounded squares began in a finite set of starts, one start would have unbounded roots. The second half of a sufficiently large square at that start contains each earlier given square at the same start, and moves that square beyond the deleted segment.

The long-repetition criterion of Adamczewski--Bugeaud--Luca, as stated by Bilu \cite[Theorem~3.3, p.~8]{Bilu}, implies that the greatest length of two disjoint equal factors among the first $N$ digits of a fixed real algebraic irrational is $o(N)$. The corresponding length $L_b(N)$ with distinct starts and possible overlap also satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-early}
 L_b(N)=o(N).
\end{equation}
Indeed, overlapping matches of length $\ell$ at distance $d<\ell$ give a period-$d$ segment of length $\ell+d$. It contains $\lfloor\ell/d\rfloor+1$ adjacent copies of a length-$d$ word. Splitting them into two equal groups gives a disjoint match of length at least $\ell/3$, as in \cite[Lemma~3.4]{Bilu}.

For a real number $x$, let $p_b(x,n)$ be the number of distinct length-$n$ factors in its canonical base-$b$ expansion, and write
\[
 H_b(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_b p_b(x,n)}{n}
\]
for the normalized factor entropy, which exists by subadditivity. The value $H_b(x)=1$ is the entropy of a normal number in base $b$. Algebraic irrationals are widely expected to be normal, but no such statement is known.

Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy-masks} shows that the threshold $1/2$ is decisive for palindromes after a rational translation. If $H_b(x)<1/2$, then finitely many nonzero rational slopes and finitely many grouping widths admit a common rational translation $\tau$ that bounds the corresponding palindrome lengths of every image $\lambda x+\tau$. The same $\tau$ can be arranged so that, on a set of grouping widths of natural density arbitrarily close to one, those palindrome lengths are at most one. If $x$ is algebraic irrational, every such image is an algebraic irrational in the same number field. In particular, the existence of a degree-$d$ algebraic irrational with $H_b<1/2$ would yield a degree-$d$ counterexample to the palindrome question in Problem~10.42. No algebraic number with factor entropy strictly less than $1/2$ is available, and the required input is not supplied by any known example.

Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy-sharpness} shows that the threshold is sharp among real inputs. For every $\varepsilon>0$ and every finite set of nonzero rational slopes, there is a set $E$ of transcendental numbers of Hausdorff dimension at least $1/2$, with $1/2\le H_b(x)<1/2+\varepsilon$ throughout $E$, such that every rational affine image of each $x\in E$ has arbitrarily long square roots and arbitrarily long even palindromes, while the maximal disjoint equal-block length and the maximal disjoint reversed-block length in a prefix of length $N$ remain $O(\log N)$.

These statements give a conditional arithmetic route to a palindrome counterexample, and a matching construction on the other side of the threshold. They do not settle Problem~10.42. Both unbounded-length questions remain open for every algebraic irrational in every integer base.
